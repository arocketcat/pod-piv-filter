Prior work: POD background removal and automatic rank selection
===============================================================

Literature check done September 24, 2026, with a direct comparison of rank rules on this package's
dataset. Items marked (not read) were found by title and listing only; their pages could not be
fetched, so nothing here depends on their content.

What already exists
-------------------

POD background removal for PIV. Mendez et al. (2017) introduced it and it is the reference method.
Their paper already includes an automatic rank rule: the smallest r such that, for every later mode,
consecutive singular values differ by less than 1% of the plateau level and the temporal mode is
orthogonal to the mean (tolerance 0.01). They also state the method works on non time resolved
sequences and that frames with different illumination should be filtered separately. Masullo's
project page (https://www.alessandromasullo.com/POD-based-Background-Removal/), journal
page (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0894177716302266), MATLAB
code (https://www.mathworks.com/matlabcentral/fileexchange/59655-pod-based-background-removal-for-particle-image-velocimetry).

Robust PCA for PIV backgrounds. Baghaie (arXiv 2019) splits the image sequence into low rank
background and sparse particles, with the standard lambda = 1/sqrt(pixels), and compares against min
subtraction and Mendez's POD. Evaluation uses image quality metrics (MSE, PSNR, SSIM), not velocity
error against a known flow. arXiv 1908.06047 (https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06047)

Other PIV background and reflection methods

-   Temporal high pass filter for time resolved data, Sciacchitano & Scarano (2014) (not read)
    IOP (https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0957-0233/25/8/084009)
-   Anisotropic diffusion for unsteady reflections, Adatrao & Sciacchitano (2019) (not read)
    IOP (https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6501/aafca9)
-   Background extraction from double frame images, Honkanen & Nobach (2005) (not read)
    Springer (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00348-004-0916-x)
-   Radon transform to detect a line of particles at a free surface and build a dynamic mask,
    Sanchis & Jensen (2011). Relevant precedent for detecting thin line features in PIV images.
    Springer (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00348-011-1101-7)
-   Treatment of light reflections in 3D PIV systems (Meas. Sci. Technol., recent) (not read)
    IOP (https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6501/ae1993)

Random matrix rank selection, the part closest to this package's rule

-   Epps & Krivitzky (2019), Exp. Fluids: fit a Marchenko-Pastur law to the tail of the singular
    values of noisy PIV velocity fields, estimating both the noise level and a spatial correlation
    parameter, then pick the rank from it. This is essentially the same idea as pod_filter.py's rule
    (Marchenko-Pastur fit with a fitted effective aspect ratio to absorb pixel correlation), applied
    to vector fields instead of raw images.
    Springer (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00348-019-2768-4)
-   Epps & Techet (2010), error threshold criterion for SVD modes of PIV data (not read)
    Springer (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00348-009-0740-4)
-   Gavish & Donoho (2014): optimal hard threshold, iid noise. arXiv
    1305.5870 (https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.5870)
-   Donoho, Gavish & Romanov (2023), ScreeNOT: MSE optimal threshold when the noise is correlated
    and its structure unknown; Python and R packages. arXiv
    2009.12297 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.12297)
-   Veraart et al. (2016), MP-PCA: Marchenko-Pastur based automatic rank for denoising diffusion
    MRI. GitHub (https://github.com/Neurophysics-CFIN/MP-PCA-Denoising)
-   Baranger et al. (2018): adaptive SVD clutter filtering for ultrafast Doppler, a structurally
    identical problem (low rank tissue clutter over noise like blood signal), with the cutoff chosen
    from the similarity of spatial singular vectors.
    PubMed (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29969408/)

Head to head on this dataset
----------------------------

Modes removed by each rule (frame A unless noted). mendez is my implementation of the criterion as
described; normalising the ones vector to unit length and taking the plateau level as the median
singular value are my choices.

  Ensemble             This package (MP fit)   Gavish-Donoho   ScreeNOT            Mendez criterion
  -------------------- ----------------------- --------------- ------------------- ------------------
  Clean, 1000 images   1                       1               1                   1
  Corrupted, 1000      10                      9               8 (7 for frame B)   10
  Corrupted, 400       9                       6               6                   61
  Corrupted, 200       7                       5               5                   198
  Corrupted, 100       6                       5               5                   88
  Corrupted, 50        5                       4               4                   49

Below 1000 images the orthogonality half of the Mendez criterion, as I implemented it, fails on
scattered bulk modes and the rule runs away; its singular value gap half alone gives 9 to 10. That
may be my reading of their normalisation, so it is not evidence against their method.

PIV error (this package's processor) against the number of removed modes, with POD fitted on N
images per cavity:

  N      Fails (RMS > 0.15 px) at   Best                 Flat within 0.01 px of best   Rules pick
  ------ -------------------------- -------------------- ----------------------------- ------------
  1000   0 to 2                     5 to 17 (0.087 px)   3 to 80                       8 to 10
  200    0 to 2                     5 to 6 (0.089 px)    4 to 15                       5 to 7
  100    0 to 2                     4 (0.095 px)         4 to 10                       5 to 6
  50     0 to 3, and 20 or more     4 to 5 (0.103 px)    4 to 7                        4 to 5

Scored on 100 pairs for N = 1000 and 50 to 60 pairs otherwise; ranks tested were 0, 2 to 10, 12, 15,
20, 25 (plus 1, 11, 14, 17, 30, 50, 80 for N = 1000).

Every rule except the runaway case lands in the flat region at every ensemble size. At 1000 images
removing extra modes costs almost nothing, but at 50 images each mode carries 2% of the particle
signal and removing 25 modes doubles the error, so "when unsure, remove more" only holds for large
ensembles.

What this means for novelty
---------------------------

Not new

-   POD background removal (Mendez et al. 2017) and robust PCA for it (Baghaie 2019).
-   An automatic rank rule for it (Mendez et al. 2017).
-   Fitting a Marchenko-Pastur law with a correlation correction to pick the rank (Epps & Krivitzky
    2019 for PIV vector fields; Veraart et al. 2016 for MRI), and correlated noise thresholds in
    general (ScreeNOT).
-   This package's rule showed no measured advantage: Gavish-Donoho and ScreeNOT pick slightly fewer
    modes and give the same or marginally lower error.

Possibly less covered (I found nothing on these, which is not proof there is nothing)

-   Scoring background removal by velocity error against an exact flow, including the tail of large
    errors. The RPCA paper used image metrics only. I could not read how Mendez et al. scored their
    tests, so check before claiming.
-   Processor dependence: the same corrupted images gave 2.0 px RMS with this package's processor,
    0.30 px with OpenPIV and 0.27 px with PIVlab, while POD brought all three back to within 2 to
    12% of their own clean result. How much artifacts hurt depends strongly on the processor; how
    well the filter works does not.
-   Non repeating thin artifacts present in both frames, where POD only partly works, combined with
    shape based detection.

Taken together, a paper would be an evaluation study (ground truth, several processors, ensemble
size, artifact types, then real data), not a new filter.
